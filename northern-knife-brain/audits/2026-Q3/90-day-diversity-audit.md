# 90-day creative diversity audit (format) — Northern Knife — 2026-Q3

## Executive summary

Northern Knife spent $337,402.58 in the 91 days from 2026-06-10 to 2026-09-08 and returned 2.24, against $142,417.77 and 1.67 in the 90 days before it. `verified` from the Parker pull of the connected account, both windows read the same way on 2026-09-08. The format story under that number is simple and uncomfortable: the account replaced a gift business with a discount business, and in doing so it collapsed from a mix built around *who is buying* into a mix built around *what the deal is*.

**Offer Based is now the account's dominant format by a distance — 41.5 percent of tagged spend and 29.5 percent of tagged groups, up from 17.4 and 22.8 percent.** `verified`. It returns 2.56 on $78,017.31 at a $60.43 cost per purchase, which is well above the account's 2.24, so nobody is wrong to be running it. Add Headline Only, which is second at 17.6 percent of tagged spend and shares 56.6 percent of its money with Offer Based, and more than half the tagged library is now a price announcement in one shape or another. The format that used to lead — Gifting, at 45.7 percent of tagged spend last quarter — fell to 13.6 percent, a thirty-two point drop and by far the largest format movement in the account.

**The under-used format with disproportionate performance is Aesthetic, and it is the most interesting number in this audit.** It holds 9.7 percent of tagged spend across 34 groups, up from 6.4 percent across 2, and returns 2.20 at a $66.32 cost per purchase. What makes it matter is not the return, it is the composition: 98.4 percent of Aesthetic spend carries "No Promotions," 100 percent is Evergreen, 97.9 percent sits behind the "to be superior" desire, and **zero percent of it is Most Aware.** `verified` from the tag-filtered pull. In an account where 93.2 percent of Offer Based spend is Most Aware and 99.8 percent is promotional, Aesthetic is the one meaningful block of money that sells the object rather than the price — and it is a seventeen-fold count increase that nobody appears to have noticed. Demo behaves the same way at 7.3 percent of tagged spend, 93.1 percent Solution Aware, 85.8 percent non-promotional, 2.16 return, and it went from one tagged group last quarter to 26.

**The format that fell out of the mix entirely is Reaction.** It carried 2.1 percent of tagged spend and five groups last quarter; this quarter it carries zero groups and zero dollars. `verified`. That is the only outright disappearance, and it lands on the exact spot the account can least afford to leave empty. The creative strategy audit already established that across all ten top-spending ads, exactly one human being speaks on camera — a red-haired man for about five seconds at the end of NK194 — and that nine of the ten cast people who appear only as b-roll under a machine voice. Reaction was the last format in the library whose whole point was a real person having a real response. Around it, every other person-led format is a rounding error: UGC Single at 0.6 percent of tagged spend, Employee Generated Content at 1.1, Founder Ads at 0.2, Authority Figure at 0.1, Before & After at $67.86. Together those five hold 2.0 percent of tagged spend.

**The format gap most worth filling is a person on camera reacting to the knife, and the lead recommendation is POV.** Fourteen named formats in the taxonomy have never carried a tagged dollar in either window, and POV is the one whose absence is loudest, because the account's closest direct rival is running it at the top of its library right now. Dalstrong's "POV: Your 14-inch Annihilator Just Arrived" sits at impression rank 9 of the tracked set, opens on a bearded man in a work apron beside a blue box on an outdoor table, and has been live 62 days. `verified` from the competitor library pull, 2026-09-08. It costs almost nothing to shoot, it is the single most native container on the platform for a premium object arriving, and it does the one job this account's format mix currently cannot do at all: it puts a face and a voice in front of the product without a deal attached.

One caution to carry into the next quarter, and it comes from the calendar rather than the tag library. Gifting is the only format in this entire account where women outnumber men — 52.0 percent of its spend reaches women, against 74.8 percent male account-wide. `verified` from the per-format demographic pull. The account is entering Q4 with its gift format cut by two thirds and its most male mix on record, in the quarter that has historically flipped this brand's buyer. That is a format decision with a season attached to it, and September is when it gets made, not November.

### Offer Based

- Count share: 152 of 515 tagged groups, 29.5 percent. Prior window: 39 of 171, 22.8 percent.
- Spend share: $78,017.31, 41.5 percent of tagged spend. Prior window: $22,030.74, 17.4 percent.
- Return: 2.56. Cost per purchase $60.43 on 1,291 purchases. AOV $154.98. CPM $12.68. CTR 1.37 percent.
- Composition: 93.2 percent Most Aware, 99.8 percent Promotions, 65.6 percent of its spend behind no identifiable desire, 73.0 percent male.
- Trajectory: rose 24.1 points of spend share. The account's largest format and its largest single movement upward.
- Per-format hook and hold rate: not available — `data-limited`, the connector exposes these per ad and only for video.

This format is doing the account's closing work, and it is doing it well. A 2.56 return against the account's 2.24, and a $60.43 cost per purchase against $70.39, means the offer ads are both cheaper and better-returning than the account average. On a pure efficiency read there is no argument to make against them. The composition is where the argument lives. Ninety-three percent of Offer Based spend sits at Most Aware — people who already know Northern Knife and are being handed a reason to act today — and 99.8 percent of it carries a promotion. This is a format that cannot introduce the brand to anybody, by construction.

Read against the brand's own stated objective, that matters. `running-notes/success-definition.md` records the objective as **scale acquisition**, `stated` by the account owner on 2026-09-07, and the note there is explicit that scaling means headroom over efficiency and net-new concepts over iterations. An account whose largest format is structurally incapable of reaching a cold audience has put 41.5 percent of its tagged money behind demand it already created. That is a harvest, not an acquisition engine, and the quarter's numbers say the harvest was rich. The question the numbers cannot answer is how much of that harvested demand was created by the *other* 58.5 percent, and whether the harvest keeps working when the creative feeding it shrinks.

One warning about counting this format, and it is verified rather than theoretical. The ad naming convention has an offer field, and it lies. `NK300`'s name carries `NA` in the offer position while its own third on-screen card reads "Buy 2 Get 2 FREE." `verified` 2026-09-08, recorded in `running-notes/brand-rules.md`. So any offer-led count taken from names alone under-counts, in a predictable direction. The 41.5 percent above comes from the AI format tag, which reads the creative, not the name — but it still sits on 55.8 percent tag coverage. Treat 41.5 percent as the best available figure and the true share of offer-led creative as at least that, probably higher.

Headline Only is the static half of the offer machine, and the overlap is not subtle: 56.6 percent of Headline Only spend also carries the Offer Based tag. `verified`. The format is one dominant line of type with a product shot and no supporting benefit list, and in this account that line is almost always a number. `P466`, the account's highest-spending static at $3,874.46, is headlined "UP TO 64% OFF!!" and returns 3.77 at a $41.66 cost per purchase. `verified` from the creative and the per-ad pull. `Z090` runs "PICK ANY 4" and returns 3.47 at $51.36. The 1.10 percent click-through is the tell that this is not a discovery format. It stops fewer people per impression than anything else running, and it converts the ones it stops at an exceptional rate, which is the signature of creative that reaches an audience already deep in consideration. The rise from 14 groups to 110 in one quarter is the team correctly noticing that these are cheap to make and cheap to convert. Nothing about that is wrong. What is worth watching is that Headline Only and Offer Based together now account for 59.1 points of tagged spend share between them — and because they overlap heavily, the honest way to say it is that a majority of the tagged library's money is in single-message price creative.

The one Headline Only ad that is not a price is `N090`, headlined "SOME PIECES DESERVE A BETTER PLACE," running scarcity through a numbered vault framing at $2,965.70 and 2.39. `verified`. It is a small demonstration that the format can carry the brand's status story rather than its discount, and there is one of it.

### Gifting

This is the single most consequential line in the audit, and it is a demographic line rather than a performance one. **Gifting is the only format in the entire account where women outnumber men.** Account-wide the window ran 74.8 percent male. Offer Based runs 73.0 percent male, Aesthetic 75.1, Demo 80.1. Gifting runs 52.0 percent female. `verified` from the per-format demographic pulls, 2026-09-08. The performance audit already established that male spend share flipped from 48.9 to 73.1 percent between the windows on a copy change from Father's Day gift creative written to a wife to grill-season creative written to the man. This format read tells you the mechanism precisely: the gender flip is the Gifting format leaving the library. It is not a targeting artefact and it is not the algorithm drifting. It is a format decision with a demographic attached.

The performance is genuinely good and genuinely different in shape. The $55.20 cost per purchase is the lowest of any major format, but the $117.07 order value is also the lowest — about $38 under the account's $154.98 offer-format average and $67 under the top-ten block's $183.41. Gift buyers here buy cheaper baskets more efficiently. Under a ROAS north star that nets out to a 2.12 return, below the account's 2.24, which is probably why nobody defended the format when the season turned. Under a scale objective it reads differently: this is the only format in the library that reaches a whole second audience, and a 2.12 return on an audience nothing else in the account touches is worth more than its ROAS line suggests.

The trajectory is where the recommendation sits. Sixty-five percent of Gifting spend is tagged Seasonal, so the fall from 78 groups to 51 is partly the calendar doing its job — Father's Day ended. But the brand-lens note is blunt about what comes next: "the account is at its most male immediately before the season that reliably makes it least male. Q4 gifting creative needs writing now, not in November." `verified` 2026-09-07. The format exists, it works, it reaches the buyer nothing else reaches, and it is currently at a third of its former size heading into the quarter it was built for.

### B-roll mashup + Voiceover

- Count share: 49 groups, 9.5 percent. Prior window: 29 groups, 17.0 percent.
- Spend share: $24,171.23, 12.8 percent of tagged spend. Prior window: $24,572.73, 19.5 percent.
- Return: 1.84 — the weakest of any format above five percent of tagged spend. Cost per purchase $72.59 on 333 purchases. AOV $133.22. CPM $13.77. CTR 1.61 percent, the highest of any major format.
- Composition: 62.0 percent Solution Aware and **21.4 percent Problem Aware, the highest problem-aware concentration of any large format in the account.** 61.9 percent male. Confidence is its lead emotion at 35.7 percent of spend.
- Trajectory: spend held almost exactly flat in dollars, $24,572.73 to $24,171.23, while its share fell 6.7 points because the account around it nearly doubled.

This is the account's house style and the reason the top-ten block sounds the way it does: real footage cut together under an independent machine voiceover, where nobody on screen is speaking the words. Every one of the ten highest-spending ads in the window uses this construction, and every one carries an `AI VO` token in its name. `verified` from the creative strategy audit's full media read of all ten.

The performance read has to hold two things at once. The 1.84 return is the worst of the account's large formats, and the $72.59 cost per purchase is above the account's $70.39. On its own that reads like a format to cut. But look at what it is being asked to do: 21.4 percent of its spend sits at Problem Aware, five times the account-wide 2.2 percent, and 62 percent at Solution Aware. This is the format doing the account's *colder* work, and colder work costs more per purchase by construction. Judging it on the same field as Headline Only, which converts people who are already three-quarters bought, is the classic misread the performance audit warned about when it said the statics look like the better buy and that read is a trap.

The 1.61 percent click-through is the number that argues for keeping it. It stops and moves more people per impression than any other format running, which is exactly what a top-of-funnel container is supposed to do. The problem is not the format, it is that the format has one execution. Forty-nine groups, one voice treatment, and — per the creative strategy audit — three of the four Feather ads inside it carrying word-for-word identical body copy. The container is working; what is going into it has stopped varying. Under the current delivery system, differentiation is what buys incremental reach, and forty-nine near-identical ads in one container is the opposite of differentiation.

This is the sliver worth more attention than the giant. Ninety-eight percent non-promotional. A hundred percent evergreen. Zero dollars at Most Aware. Ninety-eight percent of it running on status, which is precisely the desire a Viking-named, hand-forged, limited-drop brand should be running on and which the creative strategy audit already identified as the lane this account found honestly.

The return is the argument. At 2.20 it sits a hair under the account's 2.24 and comfortably clear of B-roll mashup's 1.84, and it earns that without a discount attached to almost any of it. It also carries the account's second-lowest CPM among major formats at $12.85, which says the auction likes the creative on its own merits.

The trajectory is the tell that this was probably not a plan. Two tagged groups became 34 in one quarter while nobody wrote it down anywhere in the brain — it does not appear in the creative strategy audit's format paragraph, which reported Aesthetic at 9.7 percent without noticing the count movement underneath. `verified` by comparison. A format that quietly went from two ads to thirty-four, holds its return without an offer, and is the only place the account spends money on people who are not already Most Aware, is the closest thing this library has to a second engine. It deserves a deliberate test slate rather than continued accidental growth. And it pairs naturally with the account's own scarcity truth: the Feather runs roughly 500-unit limited drops on a three-month restock, per the brand's product law in `CLAUDE.md`, which is a premium-object story that Aesthetic is the natural container for.

For a product bought for transformation and belonging rather than to solve a problem, that is not a nice-to-have format. The reaction *is* the proof of the feeling, and it is the proof this brand's own reviews are full of. The account produced five of these last quarter and none this quarter, in the same window it grew from 661 delivering groups to 1,687.

## Recommended ad formats to test

Six recommendations, ranked by conviction about fit for this brand. Every one is anchored to a real ad in Northern Knife's tracked external library. That library is a hard constraint and it needs saying before the list starts: **there is no inspo library and no affinity library for this brand.** Two brands are tracked, both direct competitors, both knife makers — Coolina USA and Dalstrong. `verified` from the tracked-brand pull, 2026-09-08. So every anchor below comes from a rival in the same category, which is good for proving a format works on this exact buyer and bad for bringing in anything the category has not already thought of. Fixing that is logged in `running-notes/missing-context.md` and is the highest-leverage thing the team could do for next quarter's version of this audit.

### 1. POV — highest conviction

**Source ad.** Dalstrong, "POV: Your 14-inch Annihilator Just Arrived." A bearded man in a heavy work apron stands at an outdoor table in daylight, a large blue Dalstrong box in front of him, the words "POV: Your 14" Annihilator Just Arrived" centred over the frame. He picks it up and says, flat and pleased, "We got another big boy. Gladiator Series 14-inch Annihilator." The knife comes out of the box at fifteen seconds and the rest of the fifty seconds is him handling it — the stand it sits on, the sheath, the weight of it in his hand — while the on-screen callouts run through what comes with it. There is no discount in the hook and no urgency in the first half; the whole opening is a man being pleased with an object that showed up at his house.

**Source library:** competitor. **Source brand:** Dalstrong. **Reference:** https://app.heyparker.ai/dashboard/ad-library?adId=2184523142400458&brandId=2ec32316-1da9-4563-a86f-d4e811e89469 — impression rank 9 of the tracked set, launched 2026-07-08, 62 days running, and re-cut into at least three further variants at ranks 23, 28 and 60. `verified` 2026-09-08.

The gap it fills is the largest single one in this audit. POV has never carried a tagged dollar in either window — it is one of the fourteen formats the account has never run — and it is the cheapest possible entry into the person-led lane that currently holds 2.14 percent of tagged spend. The format's defining feature per the taxonomy is literally the on-screen text cue plus a situational statement, which means it can be produced by one person with a phone and a box. No creator casting, no studio, no script beyond a sentence.

The lever it pulls is one the current mix has no access to: **a human being having a feeling about the product with no offer attached.** Every format the account currently leans on either announces a price (Offer Based, Headline Only), narrates over strangers (B-roll mashup), or styles the object without a person (Aesthetic, High Production). POV puts a face, a voice and a room into the frame, and it does it inside the exact moment this brand's product law is strongest — the LOKI Blackout arrives in a black branded box with a magnetic closure, which is a better opening shot than anything Dalstrong has.

The audience it unlocks is the Product Aware and Solution Aware middle that the account is currently under-serving at 4.2 and 19.8 percent of total spend. Dalstrong's POV ads are tagged Product Aware, which is the stage where somebody knows the category and is deciding between options — precisely the stage a "here is what arriving looks like" ad serves, and precisely where Northern Knife has the least creative. Conviction is highest here because the format is the cheapest to produce, has the clearest proof in the rival library, has never been tried, and lands on the account's biggest structural absence at once.

### 2. Reaction — high conviction, and it is a restart rather than a start

**Source ad.** Coolina USA, "WE HANDED STRANGERS OUR KNIFE AND SAID, JUST REACT." The hook is the line itself, spoken and stamped on screen, over an extreme close-up of a person in bright orange rubber gloves pressing a large dark blade down through a thick charred piece of meat on a star-patterned wooden board. Fifty seconds. The ad's whole argument is physical — the weight of the thing, the feel of it, people picking it up for the first time — and the offer does not arrive until thirty-six seconds in, followed by the review count, the lifetime warranty and free returns.

**Source library:** competitor. **Source brand:** Coolina USA. **Reference:** https://app.heyparker.ai/dashboard/ad-library?adId=1931883034883126&brandId=2ec32316-1da9-4563-a86f-d4e811e89469 — ranked #233 on 2026-09-01, launched 2026-08-20, 19 days running. `verified` 2026-09-08.

The gap it fills is the only outright format disappearance this quarter. Reaction held 2.1 percent of tagged spend and five groups last quarter and now holds nothing. Something was there and the team let it go, which makes this the lowest-risk recommendation on the list: the account has already proved it can produce the format.

The lever it pulls is proof of feeling. Northern Knife is not a problem-solution brand — nobody has a knife problem — so the sell runs on transformation and belonging, and agitation misfires. What works in that register is the reaction: somebody else's face doing the thing the viewer wants to feel. The brand has 4,028 reviews at a 4.93 average sitting in `audits/2026-Q3/customer-review-audit.md`, which is a large corpus of people already having that reaction in writing and nobody having it on camera.

The stage it unlocks is Problem Aware and Solution Aware, where the account is thinnest at 2.2 and 19.8 percent of total spend. A first-touch reaction ad does not require the viewer to know the brand; it requires them to want what the person on screen just felt. Conviction is high rather than highest only because it needs real people filmed, which is a bigger production ask than POV.

### 3. ASMR — high conviction, because the footage already exists

**Source ad.** Two anchors, because the format has two useful executions in the tracked library and they are different ads. First, Coolina's seventeen-second cut: a man's hands in professional black gloves working over a piece of juicy cooked steak on a premium wooden board, and one line of voice — "A lot of people ask me how sharp these Coolina knives are" — then nothing but the sound of the cut. Second, Dalstrong's sixteen-second cut, which has **no spoken dialogue at all**: text on screen reading "This belongs on display (not today)," a bearded man in a leather apron over a premium cut of beef in a rustic setting, and the blade going through it.

The gap this fills is the emptiest desire in the account. "Enjoyment of food and beverages" holds $461.44 across four ad-name groups in 91 days, of which $328.96 sits on the single ASMR-tagged group inside Demo. `verified`. The cutting footage is being shot constantly and the sensory payoff is being thrown away in favour of a voiceover explaining the deal.

The lever it pulls is the one the taxonomy says defines the format: the audio is the stop-scroll device. This account's entire library runs on a machine voiceover, which means every ad competes on the same channel — spoken words. An ASMR cut competes on a channel the account has never used, and it does so at fifteen to twenty seconds instead of the two and three minutes the top block runs. The stage it unlocks is the cold end, which is where the scale objective needs work. A silent sensory cut requires no prior knowledge of the brand and no product name. Conviction is high because the production cost is close to zero — the team is already filming tomatoes, onions, peppers, brisket and steak — and the change is a decision about sound design and length, not a new shoot.

### 4. Skit — medium-high conviction

**Source ad.** Coolina USA, the Labor Day sale ad. A bearded man in sunglasses and a grey button-down stands against a white wall hung with knife artwork and says, to an unseen second character, "Hey man, what's the Labor Day deal?" The video runs thirty-five seconds and answers itself: buy two get one free at nine seconds, a free mystery knife at fifteen, up to sixty dollars off at eighteen, then the review count, the lifetime warranty and the money-back guarantee. One creator, two roles, a conversation that gives the offer somewhere to live.

**Source library:** competitor. **Source brand:** Coolina USA. **Reference:** https://app.heyparker.ai/dashboard/ad-library?adId=1761578494894089&brandId=2ec32316-1da9-4563-a86f-d4e811e89469 — launched 2026-09-04, 4 days running. `verified` 2026-09-08.

The gap it fills is specific and unusual: Skit is a format that would let the account keep running the offer it clearly cannot stop running, while fixing the thing that is wrong with how it runs it. Offer Based holds 41.5 percent of tagged spend and 65.6 percent of that money sits behind no identifiable human desire, because a discount announcement is a transaction rather than a want. A skit does not remove the offer; it gives the offer a person to be said by and a reason to come up.

The lever it pulls is differentiation inside the account's dominant format. One hundred and fifty-two Offer Based groups currently share one voice treatment and a small set of near-identical bodies. Under the current delivery system, differentiation is what buys incremental reach — that is the account's own diagnosis in `brand-lens.md`, verified from the ad-account pull. A skit is the cheapest way to make the 153rd offer ad genuinely different from the previous 152, because it changes the delivery mechanism rather than the layout.

The stage it unlocks is Most Aware, which is already 53 percent of tagged spend — so this is not a funnel-widening recommendation and should not be sold as one. It is a fatigue recommendation. Conviction is medium-high: the format has never been run here, it requires one person willing to play two parts, and its upside is bounded by the fact that it serves the end of the funnel the account is already over-invested in.

### 5. Sceptic Test — medium conviction

**Source ad.** Dalstrong, "A $79 knife..." A man's muscular hand holds a gleaming chef's knife against a grey canvas apron, and the voice opens on the doubt directly: "A 79 dollar knife can't be good, right?" Twenty-seven seconds. It answers with restaurant durability at three seconds, twelve-hour daily use at five, German steel at eight, and then the actual argument at twelve — no luxury brand markup — before closing on a seventy-day risk-free trial.

The gap it fills is a price objection the account has never answered out loud. Northern Knife's Feather sits at $99.90 and the LOKI Blackout at $79.90, and the account's answer to price is always the same — make the price smaller by giving away more knives. The AOV read shows the account's blended order value at $157.64 for the window against $117.07 on Gifting and $154.98 on Offer Based, and every discount format is training an audience to wait for the deal. The format would have to win on the forging story, the limited drop and the lifetime warranty instead, which is a harder ad and an untested one.

### 6. Street Interview — medium conviction, and it is the swing

**Source ad.** No direct anchor exists in Northern Knife's tracked libraries — no Street Interview ad appears in either Coolina's or Dalstrong's analysed set. `verified` 2026-09-08. ## Open loops

**1. The person-led lane has never been opened, and nobody knows why.**

Across 515 tagged ad-name groups holding $188,174.74, every format built on a human being on camera adds up to 2.14 percent of tagged spend, and nine of the fourteen formats never run in either window are person-performance formats. The one format of that kind the account did run — Reaction, at five groups and 2.1 percent of tagged spend last quarter — went to zero this quarter. Nothing in `running-notes/brand-notes-from-org.md` or the intake says the brand decided against putting people on camera.

*Pull: Gap.* An absence where presence would be expected — the whole production model that puts a face in front of a product is missing from a library of 1,687 ad-name groups, and the rival library is full of it.

*Question:* What is stopping Northern Knife from putting a person on camera in its ads?

*Why it matters:* If the answer is a constraint — no creator budget, no roster, a founder who will not film — then the test slate should be built entirely from formats that work without one, and three of the six recommendations above are wrong. If the answer is that nobody has tried, the slate is right and the account has a whole production model available to it that it has never used. The two answers point at completely different quarters.

*Territory: Creators and talent.*

**2. Aesthetic grew seventeen-fold and holds its return without an offer.**

Aesthetic went from 2 tagged groups to 34, holds $18,238.66 or 9.7 percent of tagged spend, returns 2.20 at a $66.32 cost per purchase, and does it with 98.4 percent of its spend carrying no promotion, 100 percent evergreen, and zero dollars at Most Aware. In an account where 40.5 percent of all spend is promotional and 41.5 percent of tagged spend is Offer Based, this is the one large block of money that sells the object rather than the price.

*Pull: Surprise.* Given a quarter where every other taxonomy moved toward the offer, a format that grew this fast in the opposite direction is not what the rest of the context would predict.

*Question:* How much spend can the non-promotional creative in this account absorb before its return falls below the offer creative's?

*Why it matters:* The stated objective is to scale acquisition, and the note in `success-definition.md` says the useful question under a scale goal is headroom, not efficiency. If Aesthetic holds 2.20 at three times its current spend, the account has a second engine and the offer concentration is a choice rather than a necessity. If it collapses at $30,000, the offer concentration is the account being honest about what works.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**3. The account's only female-reaching format is two thirds smaller heading into Q4.**

Gifting reaches 52.0 percent female against a 74.8 percent male account. It is the only format in the library where women outnumber men. It fell from 45.7 percent of tagged spend to 13.6 percent, and 64.9 percent of what remains is tagged Seasonal.

*Pull: Tension.* The brand's own recorded history says Q4 flips this buyer, and the current format mix says the account is at its most male on record — those two cannot both be comfortable.

*Question:* Who buys Northern Knife in the fourth quarter, and how much of that buying is somebody purchasing for another person?

*Why it matters:* It decides how much of the Q4 slate is Gifting and Mashup rather than Offer Based and Headline Only, and it decides it in September, when the creative has to be written, rather than in November when the answer becomes obvious. Post-purchase surveys are empty, so nobody can currently say what share of orders are gifts.

*Territory: Personas.*

**4. High Production makes the most expensive work in the account and gets the least delivery per unit.**

Thirty-eight High Production groups earned $7,755.96 between them, an average of $204 each over 91 days, against $513 per Offer Based group. The subset of High Production that also carries the Aesthetic tag — 20 groups holding $6,053.75 — is where nearly all of its money sits.

*Pull: Pattern.* The same shape shows up in Headline plus Benefits, Infomercial, Listicle and Product Collection: more groups produced, less delivery per group, quarter over quarter.

*Question:* What is the account getting from expensive production that it does not get from cheap production?

*Why it matters:* The team's production budget is finite and this is where the most of it per ad goes. If polished work only earns delivery when it is doing the premium-object job, then High Production should be aimed at one thing and the rest of the budget moved to volume. If the answer is that the delivery gap is a message problem rather than a production one, the fix is the brief, not the camera.

*Territory: Messaging.*

**5. The two brands in the external library are both direct rivals, and both are running Unboxing and Warehouse at the top.**

Unboxing is 50 percent of Dalstrong's analysed ads and sits at impression ranks 9, 23, 28, 36 and 60. Warehouse is 31.4 percent of Coolina's and sits at rank 2. Northern Knife's Unboxing fell from 8.8 to 2.6 percent of tagged spend, and its Warehouse arrived from nothing at 3.3 percent.

*Pull: Curiosity.* Two independent rivals concentrating their libraries on the same two formats, while this account moved away from one of them, is a category dynamic the rest of Parker's context cannot yet explain.

*Question:* What is the moment of opening a knife worth in this category?

*Why it matters:* Northern Knife ships the LOKI Blackout in a black branded box with a magnetic closure, which is a stronger unboxing asset than either rival appears to have, and currently spends $4,890.29 on the format that would show it. If the rivals are right, that is a cheap and immediate correction. Impression rank is a delivery proxy and not spend, so this cannot be settled from the library alone.

*Territory: Product.*

**6. Nine hundred and eighty-eight ad-name groups have no format tag and hold 44.2 percent of the window's spend.**

Every distribution in this audit rests on 515 tagged groups holding $188,174.74 of $337,402.58. Coverage fell from 88.7 percent of spend last quarter to 55.8 percent this quarter, and the untagged remainder is $149,227.84.

*Pull: Gap.* Nearly half the money in the window has no format read on it at all, and the share with no read doubled in one quarter.

*Question:* What is running in the untagged half of this account's spend?

*Why it matters:* Every "the account has never run this" claim in this audit is a claim about the tagged set. If the untagged half looks like the tagged half, the reads hold. If it does not, the format posture described here is wrong in ways nobody can currently see. Note this is a genuine strategic question about creative composition rather than a tooling complaint — the tooling side of it is recorded in the data limitations field, where it belongs.

*Territory: Messaging.*

## Method sign-offs

This is everything I know about ad formats. The taxonomy in `parker-system/creative-strategy-context/ad-formats/` — 27 video formats, 14 static, 20 cross-modality — was used as the shared vocabulary for every format named in this doc, and formats were called exactly what the account's own AI tags call them rather than blended into invented labels. Where the account's execution sits in a gray area, the execution was described first and the closest supported format named second; that is why the long story-led offer ads are read as living between Infomercial and Offer Based rather than forced into one.

This is everything I know about reading an ad account. The two-kinds-of-metrics distinction from `ad-account-analysis.md` runs through every per-format read here: spend share and count share are delivery facts, return and cost per purchase are outcome facts, and the gap between what the team produced and what the auction funded is the finding rather than the noise.

This is everything I know about what a killer performance ad looks like. The bar in `killer-performance-ads.md` is why this audit does not treat the top of the spend leaderboard as the winners list, and why the formats that stop the most people are separated from the formats that convert most cheaply.

This is everything I know about Meta's delivery system. `andromeda-v2.md` is why differentiation, not volume, is treated as the binding constraint on an account that went from 661 delivering name groups to 1,687 while three of four Feather ads carried identical body copy.
