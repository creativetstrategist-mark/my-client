is where they land, get cut, get merged, and get ranked.

**Read the accounting before the agenda.** Sixty-four loops came in. Eight died. Thirty-two folded

not a side effect: the single biggest question in this brand was filed independently by eight of the
nine documents, each from a different angle, and seeing that stacked up is worth more than reading
any one of them alone.

**Three honest framing notes before the ranking.**

First, **Tier 1 is Parker's ranking on the evidence, not a roadmap's ranking.** Northern Knife is
mid-Phase-1. Phase 2 was never run, so no approved strategic roadmap exists in this brain and
nothing here can claim to follow one. The closest thing to a direction is the stated business

in almost every score here.

### PL-1 — Who is choosing the knife when a Northern Knife gets bought? — **17/20, Tier 1**

**Observation.** Eight of the nine documents in this brain independently noticed that two people are
involved in a Northern Knife purchase, and that every ad is written to only one of them. The account
went from 48.9 percent male spend share to 73.1 percent in one quarter, and the cause is copy, not
targeting: the prior window ran Father's Day creative written to a wife, the current window runs
grill-season copy written to the man (`verified`, `audits/2026-Q3/90-day-performance-audit.md` and
`brand-lens.md`). Gifting format, the only format in the library where women outnumber men at 52.0
percent female, fell from 45.7 percent of tagged spend to 13.6 percent (`verified`,
`audits/2026-Q3/90-day-diversity-audit.md`). Meanwhile 499 of 4,028 reviews, 12.4 percent, name a
male recipient with a possessive, and the writer is overwhelmingly a woman (`verified`,
`audits/2026-Q3/customer-review-audit.md`). Of 45 collection-language reviews read in full, 17 were
written by somebody buying for another person (`verified`,
`source-pulls/customer-reviews.md`). And on the comment side the direction flips: 177 of 1,351
comments, 13.1 percent, are one person tagging another under a knife ad with no words at all, and
the ones that do carry words read like *"Amber Holdroyd my Xmas gift"* and *"Can I have one of these
for Christmas plse Iain 😁😘 xx"* — the recipient asking, not the giver choosing (`verified`,
`source-pulls/ad-comments.md` and `personas/voice-of-customer/voc-corpus-profile.md`).

**Pull — Pattern.** The same two-people shape turned up on every surface this brain has touched:
the account's gender swing, the format mix, the review corpus, the comment corpus and the order
data. That is not one document noticing something. That is five independent surfaces describing the
same transaction from different seats.

**The question: Who is choosing the knife when a Northern Knife gets bought?**

**Why it is a loop.** Q4 creative has to be written in September, and the answer decides who it
speaks to. If the chooser is usually a woman buying for a man, the Gifting library that just got cut
by two thirds needs rebuilding now. If the user asks and somebody else pays, the ad should be built
for the person doing the asking and the money follows. Right now the brand is at its most male on
record heading into the season its own history says makes it least male, and nobody can say which
way to point. One caution the score respects: the exact count of gift orders is brand-only. The
order warehouse has no gift field and the post-purchase survey has zero responses, so every gift
figure in this brain is a language proxy or a seasonal proxy. The language proxies get you a long
way — they were already run — but the count needs one question at checkout.

*Score — Stakes 5, Confidence 5, Researchability 4, Novelty 3. **Total 17/20. Tier 1.***
Confidence is a 5 because eight documents raised it with counts and denominators attached. Novelty
is a 3, not higher, because `brand-lens.md` already records that the buyer flips seasonally; what is
new is who decides, not that two people exist.

### PL-3 — What actually separates the people who buy Northern Knife from each other? — **16/20, Tier 2**

**Observation.** The account runs a four-persona operation, encoded in every ad name: JOHN the
collector, MIKE the BBQ self-buyer, SARAH the wife buying a gift, VANCE the deal-led buyer. Meta
delivers three of them to what looks like one man. MIKE, JOHN and VANCE reached 80.4, 76.0 and 69.6
percent male and 32.7, 32.6 and 37.7 percent aged 35 to 44, across $243,242.86 of 90-day spend
(`verified`, `source-pulls/ad-account.md`). The other two names fail a different test: SARAH
separates only in a season and carries 4.9 percent of 90-day spend, and VANCE appears once in the
review corpus as a reason to buy (`verified`, `quarterly-whitespace-analysis.md`). `brand-lens.md`
already records the mechanism: product moves the reached audience about 15 points, the persona label
barely moves it at all, because a label attached to the same headline, the same opener, the same
offer and the same voice treatment does not differentiate anything.

**Pull — Tension.** The brand's own four-way map of its customer and every measurement of that
customer disagree, and the map is what every ad name, every brief and every budget line is built on.

**The question: What actually separates the people who buy Northern Knife from each other?**

**Why it is a loop.** If the honest answer is one identity with three behaviors layered on it, the
team can stop paying to write four voices and put that effort into formats, casting and occasions
instead. If it is genuinely four, the creative has to start looking as different as the labels claim.
Either way the next quarter of production changes shape. The reason this sits at Tier 2 and not
Tier 1 is plain: the observation is verified, but replacing a persona map is a project, not a pull,
and nothing in this brain has proposed what the replacement looks like.

*Score — Stakes 5, Confidence 4, Researchability 4, Novelty 3. **Total 16/20. Tier 2.***
Confidence is a 4 rather than a 5 because the delivery read is a comparison between intent and
outcome, which is the right use of the persona field but still leaves the incremental question open.

## Method sign-offs

**What this pass did.** Collected every loop from nine documents, plus the strategic questions filed
outside loop blocks in `brand-lens.md`, `running-notes/missing-context.md`,
`running-notes/brand-rules.md` and `running-notes/success-definition.md`. Ran the verdict template on

reduction came from. Re-formulated each survivor once and scored the re-formulated question, not the
original — PD-4 is the clearest example, where "which product absorbs the next $50,000" was replaced
by "which knife is actually closing the sale," because the second has to be answered before the first
can be. Scored on the four weights, routed by the rules in order with the brand-routed override
applied first.

**What this pass did not do.** It wrote no research plans, no test designs and no validation methods —
those belong to the hypothesis prompt downstream. It emitted no net-new loops; every loop above traces
