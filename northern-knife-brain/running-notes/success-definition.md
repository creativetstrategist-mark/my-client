# Success definition — Northern Knife

## The main business objective right now

**Scale acquisition.** Stated by the account owner (markalvinmagdayo@gmail.com), 2026-09-07,
during the brain set-up intake. No revenue number or deadline was attached to it.

## What that means for the work

Parker's inference, marked as such: a scale goal changes how every performance read below
ranks its findings. The question is not "what is our most efficient ad" but "what can absorb
more budget without falling apart." Concretely, that means these reads should weight:

- **Headroom over efficiency — but above the floor.** An angle running at 2.6 ROAS on $15k is a
  bigger asset than one at 3.5 ROAS on $800, because only the first has proven it survives spend.
  *Revised 2026-09-10:* this bullet originally used "2.0 ROAS on $15k" as its example of the good
  asset. That example is now wrong — at exactly 2.0 the angle contributes nothing (see the floor
  below), so volume at the floor is not an asset at all. Headroom still beats efficiency; it just
  only counts above 2.0.
- **Fatigue and diversity.** Scale dies when one creative carries the account. The diversity
  audit and the hook audit matter more under this objective than under an efficiency one.
- **Net-new concepts over iterations.** Iterating a winner protects a number; scaling needs
  new concepts entering the account at a steady cadence.
- **The efficiency floor, not the efficiency target.** With ROAS as the north star (see
  `brand-rules.md`), the useful question is how far ROAS can be allowed to fall while
  contribution still grows. **That floor now exists — see below. It is 2.0, and it changes
  the reading of this whole objective.**

## The efficiency floor — answered 2026-09-10

> **"below 2 ROAS is not profitable"** — the brand owner, asked how low the return could go
> before they'd want us to stop pushing. `stated` 2026-09-10.

This is the first profitability anchor anyone has given this brain, and it lands hard against
the account's actual numbers.

| | Figure | Against the 2.0 floor |
|---|---|---|
| Breakeven | **2.0 ROAS** | `stated` |
| Lifetime ROAS | **2.03** | **1.6% above breakeven** |
| Last 30 days | **2.18** | 9% above breakeven |

**Read that lifetime row slowly, because it reframes the objective.** Across $497,553.67 of
spend and $1,011,464.66 of purchase value, the account has cleared roughly **$8,179 above
breakeven — 0.81% of revenue** (`inferred`, arithmetic below). Over its whole life, this ad
account has very nearly exactly broken even. It has not been funding the business; it has been
paying for itself.

**The arithmetic, so anyone can check it or replace it:** a breakeven ROAS of 2.0 implies that
about **50% of revenue survives cost of goods and shipping** — that is `inferred` from the one
stated number, not a margin the brand gave us. Applying it: $1,011,464.66 × 50% = $505,732.33
of contribution, less $497,553.67 of spend, leaves **$8,178.66**. If the real contribution
margin is 48% rather than 50%, the account is *below* water lifetime. The conclusion is
sensitive to a number we still do not have, and that sensitivity is the point.

**What this does to "scale acquisition."** The objective and the floor are in direct tension,
and the tension is now the most important strategic fact in this brain:

- Scaling almost always costs efficiency. With only 9% of headroom in the current window and
  1.6% lifetime, **there is very little room to buy volume with return.** A push that drops
  ROAS from 2.18 to 2.0 does not "reduce profit" — it takes the account to zero.
- So "headroom over efficiency," the ranking rule stated above, has a hard boundary it did not
  have this morning. An angle at 2.0 ROAS on $15k is no longer straightforwardly a bigger asset
  than one at 3.5 on $800 — at exactly 2.0 it is contributing nothing, whatever the volume.
- The honest version of the objective is now: **grow spend while holding ROAS above 2.0**, not
  grow spend and watch ROAS. Every performance read in this brain should rank against that.

**One caveat that matters for how this gets applied.** Every ROAS figure in this brain comes
from Meta's own reporting under the account's 7-day-click / 1-day-view attribution, while the
brand reads performance in Triple Whale (see `brand-rules.md`). The 2.0 floor was almost
certainly stated in terms of the brand's own view of profitability, not Meta's attributed
number. **Nobody has checked whether the two tie out**, and if Triple Whale reads lower than
Meta, the true position against the floor is worse than the table above. That check is now the
highest-value reconciliation in this brain — it is logged in `missing-context.md`.
