## The brand

Northern Knife — hand-forged, Viking-inspired knives. https://northernknife.com/
(UK storefront: northernknife.co.uk, which is where the connected ad account sends traffic.)

## Product hard rules — never violate these

These are absolute. Breaking one is a factual error about the product, not a style miss.

### Feather Knife
- **$99.90.** Rosewood handle.
- The blade pattern is made **BY HAND**. `stated` by the brand owner, 2026-09-10.
- ~500-unit limited drops, roughly a 3-month restock cycle.
- Persona fit: **JOHN** (the collector).

> ✅ **RESOLVED 2026-09-10 — and the rule was the thing that was wrong.**
> Asked *"Is the feather pattern on the Feather Knife applied by laser, or is any part of it
> done by hand?"*, the brand owner answered: **"By hand."**
>
> So the previously absolute rule — *"the blade pattern is laser-applied; never say hand-etched,
> hand-finished, or engraved"* — is **retired**. It was stale, and it was stale in the direction
> that mattered: the account's five biggest Feather ads (`NK222`, `NK184`, `NK300`, `NK127`,
> `NK194`, carrying **$38,475.26**, 44.5% of top-ten spend) had been describing the pattern as
> etched and hand-made all along. **They were right. The written rule was wrong.** Nothing was
> off-brief and no copy needs pulling.
>
> **One thing this does not yet pin down.** The question offered "by laser" or "any part done by
> hand," and the answer was "by hand" — which settles that the pattern is hand-made but not
> whether *the whole pattern* or *only part of it* is hand-worked. So "hand-made," "hand-etched"
> and "takes hours to create by hand" are all now sayable. A claim that no machine touches the
> blade at any stage is a step further than the answer goes — ask before writing that one.

### BJORN Series Santoku
- Norse engravings on the blade, dragon-sculpted **brass** bolster, hand-carved wooden handle.
- **Never reference Japanese origins, and never use the word "Japanese."** Santoku is a
  Japanese-origin blade shape and the brand does not say so.
- Persona fit: **JOHN**.

### LOKI Blackout Edition
- **$79.90.** High-carbon steel, ebony handle.
- The bottle opener is built into the **handle** — not the blade, not the spine.
  **CONFIRMED 2026-09-10** by the brand owner, asked directly. This rule held, which means the
  live ad copy placing the opener in the blade (`NK222`, `NK284`) is a genuine factual error
  in-market, not an unsettled question. `NK168` and `NK219` have it right.
- Ships in a black branded box with a magnetic closure.
- An all-black remake of the #1-selling LOKI Viking Knife.
- Persona fit: **MIKE** (BBQ / grill self-buyer), **SARAH** (gift framing), and
  general or deal-led for **VANCE**.

## Copy rules that apply to everything

- **No spec-sheet jargon, ever.** No alloy codes, no HRC or Rockwell numbers, no "edge
  retention," no "blade geometry," no mm or gram specs. Say the plain material and the
  consequence instead — "hand-forged high-carbon steel… holds that edge."
- **The mechanic transfers, the words never do.** When adapting a winning ad, take its
  structure — hook type, pacing, beat order — and rewrite every sentence fresh. No verbatim
  source language, not even for an identical beat.
- **The offer resolves the subject, it never replaces it.** Whatever the hook raises, the body
  pays off *before* any offer appears. The offer lands roughly 60–80% through, never first and
  never last, and is followed by 2–4 more beats.
- **Only real offers exist:** B2G2, B1G1, and the percentage tiers. Never invent one.
- **Never reuse the identical offer paragraph** across product versions in the same batch.

## The brand-routed answers — asked 2026-09-10, answered by the brand owner

Four questions the build could not answer from any data surface were put to the brand owner as
the roll-up's brand-routed set. All four came back the same day. Recorded verbatim, because two
of them moved things that had been treated as settled.

| # | The question as asked | The answer, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the feather pattern on the Feather Knife applied by laser, or is any part of it done by hand? | **"By hand"** |
| 2 | On the LOKI Blackout Edition, is the bottle opener cut into the handle or into the blade? | **"on the handle"** |
| 3 | Did anything change with the review platform this year, and do you know why reviews stopped coming in after March 2026? | **"No idea"** |
| 4 | Roughly what does Northern Knife keep on an average order, and how low would you let the return on ad spend go before you'd want us to stop pushing? | **"below 2 ROAS is not profitable"** |

**What each one did:**

1. **Reversed a hard rule.** See the Feather Knife entry above. The rule was wrong and the ads
   were right — the opposite of what everyone assumed while the conflict was open.
2. **Confirmed a hard rule.** See the LOKI entry above. The rule was right and two live ads are
   wrong. Both contradictions resolved, in opposite directions.
3. **Answered with a blank, which is still an answer.** The brand cannot explain the March 2026
   review cutoff or the unreconciled corpus totals either. This is not deferred — it is closed
   as unanswerable from the brand side, and it means the review-platform question has to be
   settled from the platform, not from the team.
4. **Gave this brain its first profitability anchor.** Handled in full in
   `running-notes/success-definition.md` and `running-notes/brand-rules.md`.

## Named personas

The brand works in four named personas, and they are encoded directly in the ad naming
convention, which makes them readable straight off the account:

- **JOHN** — the knife collector. Fits Feather and BJORN Santoku.
- **MIKE** — "BBQ King," the grill self-buyer. Fits LOKI Blackout.
- **SARAH** — the gift buyer.
- **VANCE** — general / deal-led.

Parker's own persona work in `personas/` is built from the data independently. Where it
agrees with these four, that is corroboration; where it diverges, the divergence is
reported rather than reconciled away.
