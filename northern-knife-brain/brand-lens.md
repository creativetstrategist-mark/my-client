## Voice and copy rules the brand states

`stated`, from the team's own operating contract, 2026-09-07. Full text in
`running-notes/brand-notes-from-org.md`; the enforceable version is in `CLAUDE.md`.

- Never spec-sheet jargon — no alloy codes, HRC numbers, "edge retention," "blade geometry," mm or
  gram specs. Plain material plus the consequence.
- Never the word "Japanese," and never a reference to Japanese origins, anywhere near the BJORN
  Santoku — even though santoku is a Japanese blade shape.
- When adapting a winning ad: the mechanic transfers, the words never do. Every sentence rewritten.
- The offer resolves the subject, never replaces it. It lands 60–80% through, never first or last,
  and 2–4 beats follow it.
- Only real offers: B2G2, B1G1, the percentage tiers. Never invent one.
- Never reuse an identical offer paragraph across product versions in the same batch.

## What the data has already taught us

`verified` from the account, 2026-09-07 — the two documents this build produced.

- **The persona labels are not producing differentiated delivery.** The account runs a four-persona
  operation (JOHN the collector, MIKE the BBQ self-buyer, SARAH the gift buyer, VANCE deal-led),
  encoded in every ad name. But product moves the reached audience by about 15 points while the
  persona label barely moves it at all — because a persona label attached to the same headline, the
  same body-copy opener, the same offer and the same voice treatment does not differentiate
  anything. Differentiation is what buys incremental reach. Source: `source-pulls/ad-account.md`.

- **The audience flipped gender, and it was a copy decision.** Male spend share moved 48.9% → 73.1%
  between the prior 90 days and the current window. Copy-verified cause: the prior window ran
  Father's Day gift creative written to a wife; the current window runs grill-season copy written to
  the man. **The account is at its most male immediately before the season that reliably makes it
  least male.** Q4 gifting creative needs writing now, not in November. Source:
  `audits/2026-Q3/90-day-performance-audit.md`.

- **Iteration is eating itself.** Three of four Feather ads carry word-for-word identical body copy,
  and `NK313` — a near-copy of `NK168` — underperformed its parent on both hook rate (30.31% vs
  42.95%) and CPA ($105.78 vs $80.99). Copying a winner is not iterating on it.

- **Both top spenders are DISAPPROVED.** `NK222` and `NK184` sit in a disapproved state while
  carrying the account's largest spend lines.

- **The ad naming convention is an asset.** Fourteen fields encoding format, concept, product,
  voice, persona, offer, iteration-vs-net-new, strategist, editor, landing page and date. It lets
  any analysis read strategy directly off the account instead of guessing. Parsed in
  `running-notes/brand-rules.md`; the reading is unconfirmed by the team and worth confirming.

## Constraints Parker must respect when writing

- **Never state a profit conclusion.** Gross margin has never been provided, so every return figure
  in this brain is a revenue read. Label it as such.
- **Never treat the review corpus as representative sentiment.** 4,028 reviews at a 4.93 average is
  a heavily positive corpus; the negative tail is small and has to be sought deliberately.
- **Post-purchase surveys are empty**, so purchase *motivation* is always inferred here, never
  measured. Say so when it matters.
