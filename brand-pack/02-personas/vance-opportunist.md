## Core trigger

Vance doesn't need a knife. He needs a deal too good to ignore.

He is not browsing for a specific blade — he is scanning for the moment premium
quality and significant savings collide. When he finds it he acts fast and buys
in quantity. The multi-unit offer is his single most powerful activation
mechanism: **it gives him rational permission to spend more than he planned,
because the maths justify it.**

## Profile

- **Age** 30–60 · **male or female — the most gender-neutral avatar in the
  catalog** · offer-activated buyer: self, gift, or both at once.
- Middle class — value-conscious regardless of means.
- **Peak season:** Always-On (primary). Holiday and Father's Day amplify.
- **Hormone:** Dopamine primary (the deal delivers a hit before the product
  arrives — *"I got three for the price of two"* is a story he will tell),
  Cortisol secondary (once interested, real scarcity converts him; he has missed
  deals before and felt it).
- **NOT testosterone dominant.** Viking heritage and craft content are not his
  entry point. He absorbs quality information as *permission* — reassurance the
  deal is on something worth buying — but it is never the hook. **Lead with the
  offer, follow with the proof.**
- **NOT oxytocin dominant.** But he is the avatar most likely to buy multiple
  units for multiple people — out of logic, not warmth. The offer creates a
  multi-recipient problem he needs to solve.
- **Colour:** Yellow primary (enthusiastic, impulsive, social — he shares deals),
  Red secondary (moves immediately, doesn't deliberate or compare).

The conversion window is short. When the offer is right, the close is fast.

## Responds to / recoils from

| Responds TO | Recoils FROM |
|---|---|
| The offer stated in the first frame | Offer buried after long product content |
| "THEY DROPPED A BOMB" — event framing | Slow hooks with no value signal |
| "Instead of hiking prices like everyone else" | Vague sale language |
| Exact discount percentage stated clearly | No price or offer mentioned at all |
| Social proof by volume — safety signal | Complex or confusing offer mechanics |
| "Your husband or dad or you" — multi-recipient | Overly craft-focused, spec-heavy |
| "This offer won't stick around long" | Fake countdown timers |
| Quality proof — enough to justify the deal | Pure Viking heritage content |
| Website PDP shown — real price, real product | Lifestyle or aspirational content |
| Multiple units shown visually | Soft, slow-burn storytelling |
| "Craftsmanship over cheap disposable knives" | Aggressive hard-sell |
| Female or couple creators — gender-neutral | Single-avatar, single-gender creative |

## Language

**Words that convert:** dropped a bomb · buy 2 get 2 free · instead of hiking
prices · we made a better call · this offer won't stick around · grab it while
you still can · over 100,000 customers · **legendary** · you'll pay less · back
in stock · craftsmanship over cheap knives · your husband or dad or you · links
below

**Sentence patterns:**
- "THEY DROPPED A BOMB!"
- "Instead of hiking prices like everyone else — we made a better call"
- "You'll pay less. The knife hasn't changed."
- "Your husband or dad — or you"
- "This offer won't stick around long"
- "Grab it while you still can"
- "Craftsmanship over cheap disposable knives"
- "I checked their site yesterday — almost sold out"

**Towards-bridges:**
- "That makes buying for yourself and someone else a no-brainer"
- "That turns one purchase into three gifts solved"
- "That gives you premium quality at a price that makes sense"

**Never say "discount" or "sale"** — these words commoditise the product. Frame
the offer as a brand *decision* that rewards the smart buyer who found it.

**Bridge word:** **legendary** — it tells him the deal is on something genuinely
worth having. **"Craftsmanship over cheap disposable knives"** is the quality
anchor that validates the purchase without slowing the offer's momentum.

**Tone:** energetic, direct, slightly conspiratorial. Someone letting you in on
something before it disappears. Not a hard sell — a tip from someone who already
got theirs.
*"This is real. The deal is exceptional. I'd move fast if I were you."*

## Avoid

Leading with craft, heritage or Viking identity before the offer · burying the
offer past the first few seconds · fake urgency with no substance ·
single-avatar framing that excludes multi-unit buyers · slow atmospheric content
with no early value signal · deep specifications as the primary hook.

## ⚠️ Where this page conflicts with the process rules

and the contradiction is real — resolve it deliberately, don't split the
difference.

- **The page says: state the offer in frame one.** The copy rulebook (§C.3) says
  the offer lands **~60–80% through, never first**. Both are true statements

  explicitly described in Notion as *"the only offer-first creative in the
  Evergreen catalog"* — it is the exception, not the pattern.
  **Default to §C.3.** Deviate only on an explicit offer-led brief, and say so
  in the Creative Brief Instruction block.

  beats B2G1 by ~48% on video. Update the mechanic, keep the framing.

  copy-paste error in the source. It is Vance's list.
