# SARAH | The Wife / Partner

**Notion `Avatar` value:** `SARAH - THE WIFE`
**Focus product:** LOKI Blackout (gift framing)
**YTD:** $96,462 spend · 1.31 ROAS — near-identical budget to Vance, a third
less return. The brand's main avatar by volume and its weakest by efficiency.

> "He's hard to shop for. This is different and he'll actually use it."

## Core trigger

She finds buying for men genuinely hard. NorthernKnife breaks that pattern
because it looks impressive, feels premium, and she knows he'll actually use it.
**The gift box does half the selling for her.**

She's not buying a knife. She's buying the moment he opens it.

## Profile

- **Age** 30–58 · married or partnered · **gift buyer — highest volume**
- **Hormone:** Oxytocin primary (warmth, partnership, care — the gift is an act
  of love), Dopamine secondary (she needs the discovery hit: *"I found the
  thing"*). She shares what she finds. She sends the ad to her sister.
- **NOT testosterone or cortisol dominant.** Hard scarcity creates anxiety, not
  action. A countdown timer stresses her; a delivery guarantee reassures her.
  These are not the same thing.
- **Colour:** Green primary (buys when she trusts, relationship-first, will not
  be rushed — may see the ad three times before acting), Yellow secondary (fun,
  social, discovery-oriented; wants the gift to feel exciting to give).

She needs to see herself in the creative — another woman, in her situation, who
made this decision and it worked out beautifully.

## Responds to / recoils from

| Responds TO | Recoils FROM |
|---|---|
| Warm, personal storytelling | Hard scarcity / countdown timers |
| Another woman who found the perfect gift | Technical blade specifications |
| His reaction — seeing him love it | Aggressive urgency ("LIMITED STOCK") |
| The gift box looking beautiful | Cold, clinical product copy |
| Social proof from people like her | Celebrity or aspirational authority |
| The phrase "he'll actually use it" | Boastful brand voice |
| Gentle, knowing humour about husbands | Fear-based copy |
| Delivery deadline framing (practical) | Countdown timers (manipulative) |
| Lifetime warranty — quiet confidence | "You deserve" — she's buying for him |
| The offer as smart value, not a desperate deal | Over-polished influencer energy |

## Language

**Words that convert:** **obsessed** · stunning · perfect gift · he'll love it ·
actually use · every single time · proud · beautiful · different · thoughtful ·
surprise him · he can't stop using it · his new favourite · hand-forged · one of
a kind · he'll never put it down

**Sentence patterns:**
- "Things I wish I knew about sooner — for my BBQ-obsessed husband"
- "He's hard to shop for. This was different."
- "Now every time he fires up the grill…"
- "He actually gasped when he opened it"
- "After 22 years, I finally gave him something he LOVES"
- "Sounds like more of an excuse" *(gentle husband humour)*
- "I got him one and grabbed one for his dad too"

**Away-from bridges** — she moves away from a problem first, toward the reward
second:
- "So you never have to wonder if he actually likes it"
- "That saves you from another gift he'll never use"
- "That protects you from the awkward 'oh thanks babe' face"

**Highest-converting word:** **obsessed**. Use it early.

**Tone:** warm, conspiratorial, friend-to-friend. A woman who found the perfect
gift and cannot wait to tell you. Slightly playful about the husband. Never
salesy, never clinical.
*"I found the thing — and it worked."*

## Structure that works for her

Lead with the problem she wants to escape, land on the reward she wants to feel.

- Not "get him the best knife" — but "tired of buying him things he never uses?"
- Not "he'll love this knife" — but "he actually gasped when he opened it."

Emotion first, proof second, risk removed third. The lifetime warranty at the
close quietly kills her cortisol objection.

## Avoid

"Limited time" urgency · blade specifications unless reframed as *"why he'll be
obsessed"* · "you deserve" language · aggressive CTAs ("BUY NOW", "DON'T MISS
OUT") · influencer-coded creative — too aspirational, not relatable enough.

## ⚠️ Where this page conflicts with the corpus

- The page frames the offer as **B2G1** ("2G1 as smart value"). Storewide is
  **B2G2**, which outperforms B2G1 by ~48% on video. Verify the live offer per
  campaign (§C.3).
- The page's own reference-ad notes flag that **pure single-avatar Sarah
  targeting converts deeply but reaches no one** — the pure Sarah ad has the
  lowest ROAS (1.41) and highest CPP ($66.79) in its batch. Treat single-avatar
  Sarah briefs as a depth play to run *alongside* compilations, never instead
  of them.

**Corpus wins.**
