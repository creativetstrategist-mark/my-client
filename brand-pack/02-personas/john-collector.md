# JOHN | The Knife Collector

**Notion `Avatar` value:** `JOHN - KNIFE COLLECTOR`
**Focus products:** Feather Knife, BJORN Series Santoku
**YTD:** $45,158 spend · 1.79 ROAS — efficient and underfunded.

> "Father's Day is my excuse. I've wanted this for months."

## Core trigger

John doesn't need a reason to buy another knife. He needs a reason to buy *this*
knife. He is not upgrading his kitchen — he is adding to a collection that
defines who he is.

**Rarity is the trigger. Craftsmanship is the justification. The fear that this
specific piece will be gone before he acts is the only urgency that moves him.**

## Profile

- **Age** 25–50 · middle to upper-middle · **self-buyer, always**
- **Hormone:** Testosterone primary (identity, mastery, owning the genuinely
  rare), Dopamine secondary (he researches; new information is the reward).
- **NOT cortisol-dominant — except around genuine scarcity.** Countdown timers
  and sale pressure repel him; they signal mass-market thinking. A fixed
  production number, a sold-out stamp, a confirmed no-restock policy activates
  him sharply. He has missed a limited edition before and felt it.
- **NOT oxytocin-dominant.** Gifting language is irrelevant to his purchase.
- **Colour:** Blue primary (meticulous, reads everything, notices the difference
  between "sharp" and "razor-sharp"), Red secondary (once decided, moves fast).

The full conversion arc: **earn his respect with craft detail, then close with
real scarcity.**

## Responds to / recoils from

| Responds TO | Recoils FROM |
|---|---|
| Hard production limits ("only 500 made") | Countdown timers, fake urgency |
| "No restock. No wait list." | Mass-market language, generic claims |
| Artisan detail — etching, forging | Vague superlatives ("amazing quality!") |
| Viking heritage, historical accuracy | Gifting language of any kind |
| The blade as art, not just tool | Influencer-coded / lifestyle aspiration |
| Close-up cinematics of blade texture | Domestic kitchen settings |
| "Hand-forged" with visual proof | Discount-first or flash-sale hooks |
| Collector framing — "the last you'll ever need" | Manufactured urgency without substance |
| Sold-out proof — real PDP inventory signal | Social proof by volume alone |
| First-person collector voice | Over-polished brand voice |
| Outdoor, fire, nature — primal aesthetics | Soft, warm, harmony-centred content |

## Language

**Words that convert:** hand-forged · limited edition · only 500 · feather
etching · artisan · Viking · Valhalla · collection · once these are gone · no
restock · no wait list · insane detail · light as air · sharp as legend ·
heirloom · last knife you'll ever need · crafted for life · rare · one of a kind
· forged in fire

**Sentence patterns:**
- "I almost didn't get one of these"
- "They only made 500 of them. That's it."
- "Once these are gone, they're gone. No restock. No wait list."
- "Light as air, sharp as legend"
- "It looks like it's been forged in the fires of Valhalla itself"
- "I checked their site yesterday — almost sold out"
- "Beautiful pieces that will eventually become family heirlooms"

**Towards-bridges** (his motivation is *toward* an identity, not away from a
problem):
- "That makes every knife you own before it feel ordinary"
- "That gives you something most people will never hold"
- "That turns a tool into something worth passing down"

**Highest-trust words:** **insane** (applied to craft detail — signals genuine
amazement, not marketing polish) and **heirloom** (his deepest identity word).

**Tone:** reverent, precise, slightly awestruck. A collector who has just found
something extraordinary and needs to tell you before it disappears.
*"I found something rare. I almost didn't get one. You need to see this."*

## Avoid

Gift-framing · discount-first hooks · countdown timers · volume social proof as
the primary trust signal · domestic-kitchen-only settings · generic performance
claims with no artisan specificity.
