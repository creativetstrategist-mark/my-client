# NorthernKnife — Seasonal Copy Format Library
*Built from the Holiday Sale copy bank. Treat as reusable FORMATS, not holiday-only content —
swap in 4th of July, Father's Day, BBQ season, etc. wherever "Holiday/Christmas" appears.*

## Format 1 — Testimonial-style
Visual: festive/seasonal tweet bubbles, UGC-style quote cards.
Examples: "Best gift I've ever given. He hasn't stopped cooking since." / "Sharper than anything under the tree." / "Finally, a gift that doesn't end up in the closet."
Headline frames: "Real Reviews From 10,000+ Happy Cooks" / "The Gift They'll Thank You For Every Time They Cook"

## Format 2 — Compared-At (side-by-side)
"Socks & sweaters" vs. "A knife they'll brag about forever"
"$20 kitchen set" vs. "Heirloom-worthy steel"
"Forgettable gift card" vs. "A gift they'll use every day"
"Mass-produced junk" vs. "Hand-forged craftsmanship"
"The knife he hides in the drawer" vs. "The knife he shows off at every BBQ"
*(Swap the comparison object — sweater, wallet, belt, perfume, cologne — to fit the season/occasion.)*

## Format 3 — "If This Is Him…" (occasion-triggered, 3 variants: "If he," "If your man," "If your dad")
BBQ/Meat: "If he loves BBQ, gift him the knife built for it." / "If he smokes brisket on weekends, he needs this blade."
Cooking/Kitchen: "If he thinks he's the chef, gift him steel that proves it." / "If cooking is his therapy, this knife is his cure."
Humor: "If he says 'I don't need anything,' he actually needs this."
Masculine/Legacy: "If he values quality, gift him forged steel that lasts forever."

## Format 4 — Search-style hooks ("ChatGPT-style")
"What's the best Christmas gift for my husband who loves BBQ?"
"What should I get my man if he already has everything?"
"What can I buy for Dad that he'll actually use every day?"
*(Adapt occasion: birthday, anniversary, "just because," housewarming, etc.)*

## Format 5 — Note-style ad (long-form, founder/brand voice)
Structure: attention-grabbing headline → reframe the product as more than a gift/deal → state the offer → soft urgency line → sign-off ("— NorthernKnife" or "— Andy, Founder").
Five proven angle variants: Gifting, Scarcity, Legacy, Humor, Urgency. Each follows the same shape — open big, deliver the offer mid-way, close on a real (not fake) urgency line.

## Format 6 — iMessage-style (real chat vibe)
Two-person text exchange, casual lowercase tone, ends with one person suggesting buying extra units because of B2G1/B2G2.
Keep the texting voice authentic — lowercase, abbreviations ("af," "fr," "lol"), emoji used sparingly and naturally.

## Format 7 — Late Notice / Founder's Note (scarcity-driven, use sparingly)
Five variants: Holiday Stock Notice, Founder's Note, Urgent Price Angle, Apology + Scarcity, Legacy Angle.
All follow: acknowledge unexpectedly high demand → reaffirm the brand chose not to raise prices → state the offer → close on real urgency, signed by brand or founder.
**Use real, brand-consistent urgency only — never a fabricated countdown.** This format works because it reads as an honest update, not a sales tactic; overusing it burns that trust.
