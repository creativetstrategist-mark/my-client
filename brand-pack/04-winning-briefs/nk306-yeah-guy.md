## Creative Brief Instruction

- Generate **two different male VOs** on Elevenlabs — one for the customer, one
  for us.
- **Splitscreen dialogue:** customer on the left, subtitle colour red. Us on the
  right, subtitle colour green. Our side is deadpan and flat the whole way
  through.
- Every `Yeah.` is one cut. Hold it short. The comedy is the flatness — no
  reaction, no dead air.
- **Generate ONE `Yeah.` take and reuse that exact audio file for every beat.**
  Do not regenerate it per line — identical delivery every time is the joke, and
  one wrong inflection out of twelve kills it. Same for `Yeah?` in the hook: one
  take, reused.
- The single `No.` is the only break in the pattern. Land it harder than the rest.

> 💡 For VO generation on elevenlabs use the V3 model and click enhance to inject
> emotion tags.

## What to take from it

- **The return-reversal hook.** "I'm sending it back — not because it's bad."
  The second sentence turns against the first (§C.2). All three variants are the
  same turn executed three ways.
- **Offer placement.** The deal enters at beat 2 of 12 as a *question the
  customer asks*, and is not resolved until beat 7 ("five blades, I pay for
  three"). Four more beats follow before the close. The offer resolves the
  subject; it never replaces it (§C.3).
- **The `No.`** One break in a twelve-beat pattern does more work than any
  amount of emphasis. The pattern has to be genuinely rigid for the break to
  land — hence the instruction to reuse one audio file.
- **Product truth in passing.** "bottle opener sitting right in the handle" —
  correct per §E, and delivered as a consequence ("I cut steak and popped a beer
  without setting it down"), never as a spec.
- **The close** is the fixed shape: CTA → proof stack → parting jab that loops
  back to the hook ("Don't be the guy sending his back").
