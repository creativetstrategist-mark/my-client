## What to take from it

- **The offer lands at beat 9 of 12** — roughly 70% through, exactly the §C.3
  window — and four beats follow it. The hook asks "why is this so expensive";
  the body answers it completely before the offer appears. The offer is the
  payoff, not the argument.
- **"So no, we didn't drop the price."** The brand refuses the premise rather
  than defending its pricing. Compare the delete-on-sight rule against sentences
  that explain our own pricing to the viewer — this one explains where the money
  *goes*, which is a different move.
- **Craft explained without a single spec-sheet term.** The stamping-versus-
  forging passage is the longest technical stretch in any brief here and
  contains no alloy code, no HRC number, no millimetre. "Hammering hot steel
  packs the crystals inside it smaller and tighter" is the §C.1 formula at full
  length: plain material + consequence.
- **Iteration discipline.** The brief names exactly where the proven script
  starts and forbids touching it. When you re-front a winner, mark the seam.
- **The self-critical Note column.** 3D's note flags that the hook promises
  maths the body doesn't deliver, and proposes the fix. Notes are for real
  production risk, not praise.
- **VANCE leads with the event, never the mechanic** — even here, where the
  whole ad is about price.
