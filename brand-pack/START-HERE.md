## The three rules that get work rejected

Read these before you write a single line. They are not style preferences —
scripts get sent back for breaking them.

**1. No spec-sheet jargon. Ever.**
No alloy codes (`5Cr15MoV`), no HRC or Rockwell numbers, no "edge retention",
"blade geometry", gram weights, or millimetres. An audit of 40 real briefs found
zero occurrences. Real copy is *plain material noun + consequence*:

> "hand-forged high-carbon steel… holds that edge."

**2. The offer resolves the subject — it never replaces it.**
Whatever the hook sets up must be paid off by the body **before** the offer
appears. The offer lands around 60–80% of the way through — never first, never
last — and is always followed by 2–4 more beats.

The most common failure is talking about the problem and then switching
abruptly to the discount. That reads as a different ad bolted on.

**3. The mechanic transfers; the words do not.**
When you adapt a winning ad, take its *structure* — never its language. Even
when the beat is identical, write it fresh. This is the single most-repeated
correction in this project's history.

## How to calibrate

Read 3–4 briefs from `04-what-works/real-briefs/` before writing anything.

The real register is **blunter and more fragmented than feels safe to write.**
Reading real ones is the only reliable way to internalise it. Writing from the
persona docs alone reliably produces copy that is too polished and too soft.

One trap: house style has moved on. Hooks are now a 2–3 sentence setup-then-turn,
roughly 19–30 words — not the short one-liners in some older briefs.

Another: an offer *tag* on a brief does not mean the offer goes *in* the script.
Some of the highest-performing ads carry an offer tag and never state an offer.

## Two things deliberately not in this pack

**Performance data.** No revenue, ad spend, ROAS, margins or forecasts. Where a
brief mentions an ad was a strong performer, the judgement is kept and the figure
removed. You do not need the numbers to write the creative, and they are
commercially sensitive.

**The ad media itself.** The written analysis of each winning ad is here; the
actual videos and images are not.
