# Product catalog — the 3 focus products

Everything a script is allowed to claim is on this page. If a detail isn't
here, it isn't verified — don't say it, don't imply it, don't soften it into
something sayable.

Only these three products. Don't substitute other catalog items unless the
user explicitly asks.

---

## Feather Knife

**Price:** $99.90
**Handle:** rosewood
**Blade pattern:** **laser-applied**
**Availability:** limited drops of ~500 units, restocks roughly every 3 months
**Persona fit:** JOHN (collector)

### Never
- Never say the pattern is **hand-etched**, **hand-finished**, or **engraved**.
  It is laser-applied. This holds for spoken lines, captions, and any
  paraphrase that implies hand work on the pattern.

### Say
- "The pattern's laser-applied — dead clean, every single one."
- Rosewood by name. It's the sensory detail.
- The drop size and the ~3-month restock are true scarcity. Say them plainly;
  don't dress them up and don't invent a date.

### How JOHN hears it
Consistency is the pitch. A collector wants the pattern identical across the
drop — laser-applied is the *reason* it is, so it's a feature, never an
apology.

## BJORN Series Santoku

**Blade:** Norse engravings
**Bolster:** dragon-sculpted **brass**
**Handle:** hand-carved wood
**Persona fit:** JOHN (collector)
**Price:** `TODO(brand-pack)` — not in the hard-rules set; confirm before any
script prices this product.

### Never
- **Never use the word "Japanese."** Never reference Japanese origin,
  tradition, technique, or heritage, directly or obliquely. The santoku is a
  Japanese-origin knife shape and the brand never says so. This includes
  "Eastern", "Japan-inspired", and naming the shape's origin in any form.

### Say
- Norse engravings, dragon-sculpted brass bolster, hand-carved wooden handle.
- Describe the shape by **what it does on a board** — never where it's from.
  "Flat edge, so it actually meets the board" is fine. "Japanese-style
  santoku" is not.
- The brass bolster and the carved handle are the hand-work beats here (unlike
  the Feather Knife pattern).
