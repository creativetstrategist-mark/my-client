# B-roll library

Every batch ships **exactly 8 shared b-roll clips**, 2–4 seconds each, all
shootable by the same creator in the same session. This library is the pool to
pick from. Keep at least one **cut** clip and one **material** clip in every
batch.

## The standard 8

1. **Reveal** — out of the box, or out of the block.
2. **Material close-up** — the named material: rosewood, ebony, or brass.
3. **Signature detail** — pattern, engraving, or bolster, tight and still.
4. **The cut** — the knife doing its actual job, close.
5. **Hand on handle** — scale and grip.
6. **The second use** — grill, board, or the LOKI handle opener.
7. **Context wide** — the knife in the room it lives in.
8. **Pack-out / gift beat** — the box, the magnetic closure, the hand-off.

## Per product

### Feather Knife
- Clip 2: rosewood grain, raking light.
- Clip 3: the laser-applied pattern, phone propped, dead still. This clip is
  the product's argument — consistency — so it has to be sharp.
- Clip 8: drop packaging. **Never** a shot staged to imply hand-finishing of
  the pattern — no engraving tools, no workbench-with-the-pattern framing.

### BJORN Series Santoku
- Clip 2: hand-carved handle, showing the carving.
- Clip 3: dragon-sculpted brass bolster with soft side light.
- Clip 4: flat-edge cut meeting the board — herbs or an onion.
- **Never** a styling choice that signals Japanese origin: no Japanese
  ceramics, calligraphy, kanji packaging, sushi, or sashimi plating.

### LOKI Blackout Edition
- Clip 2: ebony handle **against a light board** — it vanishes on dark.
- Clip 6: the bottle opener, framed so the **handle** is unmistakably the
  opener. Never cut it to look like the blade or spine does the opening.
- Clip 8: the black box, magnetic closure snapping shut. This is the gift beat.
